Real-time glass, in five terms.

Iridescence — A thin film on the surface that shifts hue with the viewing angle, like soap bubbles or coated lenses. It is driven by a film thickness range and the film's own index of refraction.

Index of refraction — How strongly light bends as it enters the material. Water sits near 1.33, glass near 1.5 and diamond at 2.42; higher values pull reflections toward the edges.

Dispersion — How the index changes across wavelengths, so each color bends a little differently and bright edges split into faint spectra. KHR_materials_dispersion expresses it as an Abbe number.

Transmission — The share of light that passes through the surface instead of bouncing off it. Thickness and attenuation then tint and darken what is seen through deep glass.

Roughness — Microscopic surface variation that scatters reflected and transmitted light. Zero is a perfect polish; higher values frost the glass and soften everything behind it.
